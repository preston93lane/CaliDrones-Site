What you get

One flight, several outputs: a map you can measure, a model you can orbit, and the numbers you need.

  • Orthomosaic maps
  • Point clouds

A mapping flight follows a planned grid with heavy photo overlap, flown with a DJI Mavic 3 Enterprise built for mapping work. The images are processed into an orthomosaic (a single, scaled top-down image of the whole site), a point cloud and a textured 3D model.

Deliverables come in standard formats that open in common GIS, CAD and construction software; the exact formats are agreed in the quote. Stockpile and cut-and-fill volumes are reported per pile or area with the method noted.

Accuracy, stated plainly

Drone maps are excellent for planning, progress and quantities. When a project needs survey-grade results, we fly on ground control set by your licensed land surveyor, who checks and certifies the work, as California law requires for surveying.

Questions

What is an orthomosaic?

A single top-down image of a whole site, stitched from hundreds of photos and corrected so distances are true to scale. You can measure lengths and areas directly on it.

Is a drone map the same as a survey?

No. Drone maps are well suited to planning, progress and quantities. For survey-grade work, we fly on ground control set by your licensed land surveyor, who checks and certifies the results.

How big an area can you map?

From a single lot to large sites. Bigger areas take more flight time and processing, and are planned so each part of the site is captured with the same overlap.

What file formats do you deliver?

Standard formats that open in common GIS, CAD and construction software, for example GeoTIFF orthomosaics, LAS or LAZ point clouds and OBJ models. The formats are confirmed in your quote.

Can you measure stockpile volumes?

Yes. We fly the stockpiles, build a surface model and report volumes per pile or area. Regular repeat flights let you track changes over time.

Can you fly in controlled airspace?

Yes. Many Los Angeles sites are near airports. We request LAANC authorization, and further FAA coordination where it's required, before the flight.
